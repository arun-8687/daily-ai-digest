By Hermes·Executive Summary

The Day in Review

Two competing narratives dominate the AI developer productivity landscape this week. On one hand, cloud-based agents are going mainstream — Gergely Orosz's firsthand reporting from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Cursor offices reveals that all three labs are betting heavily on cloud-hosted AI agents that require zero local setup, with Claude Managed Agents emerging as a six-month engineering bet at Anthropic. At OpenAI, over 95% of non-engineers now use Codex rather than ChatGPT, signaling that coding harnesses are becoming the universal AI interface even outside engineering teams.

Simultaneously, the geopolitical landscape for AI is fragmenting. The US government's sudden restriction of Anthropic's Mythos-class models has accelerated a flight to open-weight Chinese models. Jack Clark's Import AI reports Claude Fable achieving an astonishing 18.71× speedup writing GPU kernels autonomously — a concrete signal that AI systems are beginning to perform the foundational R&D tasks needed for recursive self-improvement. The contrast between these accelerating capabilities and the political constraints on their deployment creates the central tension for engineering leaders planning their AI strategy.

By Gergely OroszWhy read: This is the most grounded, firsthand report on where the three leading AI labs are placing their engineering bets. Essential reading for any leader planning agent infrastructure investments.
Author: Gergely Orosz / The Pragmatic Engineer

1. Impressions from visiting OpenAI, Anthropic, & Cursor

Based on onsite visits, Orosz identifies four trends: (1) cloud agents as the next mega-trend with all three labs going all-in; (2) 95%+ of non-engineers at OpenAI now use Codex over ChatGPT — coding harnesses are spreading beyond developers; (3) the main work of engineers is shifting to building environments for agents; (4) companies are aggressively optimizing spend-per-token (Coinbase case study). Karpathy's "Claude Tag" Slack integration is less important than what it represents — agents running in the cloud with zero setup.

"My sense is that the excitement here is less about the Slack integration itself, and more to do with the fact that it's easy to kick off one or more AIs that no longer run on a local machine. You can skip the setup entirely."
By Ethan MollickWhy read: Mollick provides the data every CIO needs to justify AI investment — and the framework to understand why the old "chatbot" mental model is dangerously obsolete.
Author: Ethan Mollick / One Useful Thing

2. The twilight of the chatbots

Mollick documents accelerating AI capability gains with concrete data: Epoch found Opus 4.7 working alone for 14 hours built a software package requiring 2-17 weeks of human engineering work (cost: $251 in tokens). Mollick's own experiments with Fable showed it working autonomously for 9 hours on complex software projects "that would have taken a team well over a week." The dominant paradigm is shifting from "co-intelligence" (humans guiding AI step-by-step) to autonomous agents that handle entire tasks. Open-weight Chinese models are climbing their own exponential curve, 6-12 months behind the frontier.

"As AIs can do longer and longer tasks, the way people are using AI is changing. Until recently, the dominant way to use AI was as a co-intelligence... But that is changing as AIs can do things that take more than a few hours."
By Ali El-ShayebWhy read: A concrete playbook for engineering leaders being asked to scale content output while controlling headcount. The framework applies beyond content to any knowledge-work pipeline.
Author: Ali El-Shayeb / Islands

3. How to scale content creation without adding headcount

El-Shayeb argues that most startups are stuck in the "assistant phase" — giving writers AI tools but keeping humans at the center of the bottleneck. The real shift is to "agentic content supply chains" where multi-agent orchestration systems handle research, drafting, and generation without constant human intervention, reducing manual drafting from 100% to under 10%.

"When you add headcount to solve a volume issue, you aren't just adding talent. You are adding a management tax. You are adding more Slack channels, more revision cycles, and more institutional friction."
By Gergely OroszWhy read: Beck's perspective frames the AI productivity paradox: as code generation accelerates, the bottleneck shifts to trust, testing, and verification. Engineering leaders need to rethink their quality frameworks.
Author: Gergely Orosz / The Pragmatic Engineer (Podcast)

4. How Kent Beck shapes the software engineering industry

Kent Beck argues that the software industry is "failing to accumulate trust during this new era at the same high rate as new code is being accumulated." He introduces an "explore, expand, extract" framework for navigating major technology shifts and emphasizes that building trust — not just generating code — will define the future of software engineering in the AI era.

"Building trust—not just generating code—will define the future of software engineering in the AI era."
By Justin JohnsonWhy read: This is the clearest articulation yet of the "harness is the moat" thesis that applies to every AI-for-science and AI-for-engineering product. Leaders should invest in the orchestration layer, not get locked into any one model provider.
Author: Justin Johnson / Run Data Run

5. Claude Science and the boring 80 percent

Johnson dissects Anthropic's Claude Science launch and argues the real insight is architectural: "The model is the commodity layer. The harness around it is the moat." Claude Science doesn't ship its own protein-folding models — it orchestrates NVIDIA's BioNeMo, Evo 2, and OpenFold3. Three design decisions matter: reproducible figures (code + environment bundled), agent-managed compute, and tooling that Anthropic doesn't own.

"The model is the part of your AI strategy you can order off a price sheet. The thing you build around it is the part nobody can copy."
By Colin MatthewsWhy read: The PM-as-builder trend has direct implications for how engineering teams structure themselves. If PMs can prototype and ship with AI tools, the traditional engineer-PM handoff changes fundamentally.
Author: Colin Matthews / Lenny's Newsletter

7. Import AI 464: Fable writes GPU kernels; AI automation; and analog computation

Two must-read data points: (1) Claude Fable achieved an 18.71× speedup writing CUDA kernels on Blackwell hardware (vs. 14.4× for Opus 4.8 and 4.34× for GPT-5.5) — the first genuine megakernel on KernelBench-Mega. (2) The Remote Labor Index (RLI) shows AI success rate on online freelance projects jumping from 2.5% (Oct 2025) to 16.1% (Jul 2026), with Fable 5 far ahead of GPT-5.5 (6.3%) and Opus 4.8 (8.3%).

"The frontier has more than quadrupled in under eight months, a concrete signal of how quickly economically capable AI agents are advancing."

8. The Token Apocalypse

The US government's restriction of Anthropic's Mythos models on June 12 has triggered a major shift from closed-source to open-weight Chinese models. Spencer notes "vibe coding failed to contribute meaningful products at scale" in 2025, and 2026 is seeing "token-minimization" as AI agents fail to demonstrate as much value as hoped. The buildout of "Mega Neo Clouds" — SpaceX, Meta, Softbank all entering compute-as-a-service — is accelerating despite the productivity doubts.

"If 2025 was the year where vibe coding failed to contribute meaningful products at scale, 2026 is the year where tokemaxing led to high Claude code bills and became token-minimization where AI agents failed to demonstrate as much value as it was hoped."

Trending Themes

  • Cloud agents go mainstream: Multiple sources (Orosz, Karpathy via Orosz, Ben's Bites) confirm that the next wave is agents running in the cloud, not on developer machines. Zero-setup, long-running agents hosted by providers are the architectural bet at all three major labs.
  • The model is the commodity, the harness is the moat: Johnson (Run Data Run) and the Databricks Omnigent episode (Latent Space) both hammer this point — competitive advantage comes from the orchestration layer, not the model.
  • Capability acceleration vs. value capture gap: Mollick, Clark, and Spencer all document rapid capability improvements (18× GPU kernel speedups, 16% freelance task automation, software projects in hours vs. weeks) while simultaneously noting that enterprise ROI remains uneven and trust is eroding.
  • Geopolitical fragmentation of AI supply: The US government's restriction of Anthropic's Mythos models is accelerating adoption of Chinese open-weight models (GLM-5.2 from Z.ai, DeepSeek V4), creating a bifurcated market.
  • Non-developers becoming AI-native builders: OpenAI (95% of non-engineers on Codex), Lenny's Newsletter (PMs prototyping with code), and the broader "coding harness" trend all point to a world where the primary interface to AI is code, not chat.
By Hermes·For Leaders

CIO Action Item

Run a 30-day "agent infrastructure audit" focused on two things: (1) identify where your engineering teams are still using AI as "assistants" (human-in-the-loop on every step) vs. autonomous agents that can be delegated end-to-end tasks — the gap between these modes is where the 10× leverage lives; (2) assess your dependency on any single model provider, particularly closed-source US frontier models that face regulatory risk — the shift to open-weight models (GLM-5.2, DeepSeek V4) and the "harness is the moat" philosophy mean your orchestration layer should be model-agnostic. Invest engineering time in building abstraction between your agents and the models they call. The labs themselves are betting on this architecture; your teams should too.
