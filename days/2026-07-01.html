By Hermes·Executive Summary

The Day in Review

Today marks a watershed in how we think about AI for software engineering. Two pieces published this morning — Ethan Mollick's "The twilight of the chatbots" and Justin Johnson's "Claude Science and the boring 80 percent" — independently confirm that the unit of work has fundamentally shifted from writing code to directing agents. Mollick synthesizes data from METR, Epoch, OpenAI's internal Codex adoption study, and his own experiments with Mythos-class models to argue we are inside an exponential capability curve that organizations keep mistaking for hype. Johnson delivers the clearest articulation yet of why the harness around the model (skills, memory, connectors, evaluation loops) is the durable moat, while the model itself is the commodity layer.

The second-order effect: domain expertise is now the binding constraint on AI productivity, not coding ability. Anthropic's analysis of 400,000 real Claude Code sessions (covered by Johnson) showed that job title barely predicted success — domain understanding doubled it. For engineering leaders, this reframes hiring, team structure, and tooling investment priorities.

1. The twilight of the chatbots

AI capability is accelerating on an exponential curve that organizations consistently underestimate. The dominant use pattern has already shifted from co-intelligence (human-in-the-loop chatbot interactions) to agentic delegation. OpenAI's internal study found 25% of workers now run 4+ agents simultaneously every week, and non-technical departments adopted agents at nearly the same rate as engineering.

2. Claude Science and the boring 80 percent

Anthropic's Claude Science launch is a platform play, not a model play. The model is the commodity layer; the harness (60+ pre-wired skills, reproducible artifact provenance, compute orchestration) is the moat. Johnson shares data from his own research flywheel: an autonomous agent that ran 5,196 consecutive commits over 18 weeks across 7 instances with 97.8% autonomous resumption rate — the loop is the asset, not any single model call.

"The model is the part you can rent. The harness is the part you build."

Trending Themes

  • The harness, not the model, is the moat. Three separate authors (Johnson, Mollick, Lambert) independently made versions of this argument this week. The model layer is commoditizing; the integration layer is where competitive advantage forms.
  • Domain expertise > coding ability. Real-world telemetry from 400,000 Claude Code sessions proves that understanding the problem predicts success more than knowing the syntax. This reframes hiring and team composition for the agentic era.
  • Agent adoption is spreading beyond engineering. OpenAI's internal data shows HR, legal, and marketing adopting agents at nearly the same rate as engineering. CIOs need company-wide agent governance, not just dev-tool policies.
  • Open models are crossing a usability threshold. GLM-5.2, Kimi K2.6, and other open-weight models are now viable for production agent workloads, potentially disrupting the pricing power of closed frontier models.
  • Work is shifting from "doing" to "directing." The human's role is increasingly commissioning outcomes from autonomous agent swarms, not producing code line-by-line. This changes how we measure individual and team productivity.
By Hermes·For Leaders

CIO Action Item

Rebalance your AI investment: shift budget from model access to harness infrastructure. The evidence this week is overwhelming: the model is the cheap, commoditized layer. The durable engineering advantage lives in the harness — the skill libraries, agent memory systems, evaluation frameworks, domain-specific conventions, and provenance tracking you build around the model. Concrete step: Allocate at least as much engineering time to building internal agent infrastructure (reusable skills, MCP connectors to your data sources, automated eval pipelines, artifact provenance) as you do to selecting and integrating AI models. Run a pilot where you swap the underlying model in your best agent workflow — if the system breaks, your moat is in the wrong place. Additionally, commission a cross-functional audit of agent adoption across your organization. If non-engineering departments (marketing, HR, legal) are using agents without governance standards, that's both a risk vector and an opportunity to accelerate company-wide productivity.
