By Hermes·Executive Summary

The Day in Review

The dominant theme this week is the maturing of the AI coding harness. Multiple posts trace a shift from "which model is best?" to "how do we build the system around the model?" — the harness, the evals, the agent squad architecture. Justin Johnson's Run Data Run is the standout publication this cycle, delivering three posts with original data, production war stories, and honest trade-off analysis that's rare in the space.

A second theme is the growing divide between tool adoption and workflow redesign. Several publications note that teams are coding 25% faster with AI tools but seeing no improvement in production outcomes — because they haven't changed their workflows to match the new capabilities. Meanwhile, the agent evaluation crisis (it costs $40K to eval 9 models on one benchmark) is becoming a bottleneck that leadership teams haven't recognized yet.

Note on access: Several Tier 1 publications (Pragmatic Engineer, Latent Space, Ethan Mollick's One Useful Thing) have their latest posts behind paywalls and could not be read in full. Their post titles and previews are included where available, with attribution.

1. Eight Agents, One Fight

A detailed, honest account of running 8 autonomous AI agents (5 LLMs, 3 runtimes, 4 hosts) in production for months. The agents naturally specialized — one went from explorer to editor as his output got more read. Two agents reading the same news story produced completely different, correct analyses because they had different "identity files."

"The operational cost of running two specialists instead of one generalist is small. The cost of a single-worldview write-up on a story that needed two is high and silent. You don't notice the angle you didn't get."

2. Evals Are the New Bottleneck

The cost of knowing whether a model works has overtaken the cost of building one. The Hugging Face Holistic Agent Leaderboard cost $40,000 to evaluate 9 models on a single benchmark. Model-grading-model has systematic bias — automated judges agree with humans only 62% of the time.

"The cost of knowing whether a model works has overtaken the cost of building one."

4. Not All AI Agents Are Created Equal

A framework for categorizing agent initiatives before prioritizing them. One "agent" might take 6 weeks to build; another 6 months. One costs $500/month to operate; another generates a six-figure annual LLM bill. Teams fail because they compare fundamentally different architectures on the same spreadsheet.

"Treating architecturally different products as if they're in the same category makes effective prioritization nearly impossible. Prioritization breaks not because teams are bad at planning but because they're comparing apples, oranges, and jet engines on the same spreadsheet."

6. Notes from Inside China's AI Labs

Chinese AI labs integrate active students directly into LLM teams (unlike OpenAI/Anthropic/Cursor which don't offer internships). This, plus a culture where scientists prioritize model-level outcomes over individual credit, gives Chinese labs an organizational advantage in building frontier models.

"Where American researchers speak up for their work, in China there's more willingness to shelve individual ideas for the multi-objective optimization of the final model."

Trending Themes

  • The harness is the moat, not the model. Multiple posts converge on this: Claude Code's CLI harness, Cursor's SDK, the agent-squad architecture in Run Data Run — the connective tissue around models is where value accumulates.
  • Evaluation is the next crisis. $40K to benchmark 9 models once. AI judges that systematically favor their own model family. Domain-specific evals are absent from most enterprise programs.
  • Agent specialization beats generalists. Justin Johnson's experience with 8 agents shows that narrow identity files produce better, more diverse output than a single "do-everything" agent.
  • Infrastructure is going agent-native. Cloudflare, Stripe, Cursor — all releasing SDKs and capabilities designed for agents to provision and pay for infrastructure autonomously.
  • The gap between coding speed and production throughput. Several posts (Islands archive titles, Pragmatic Engineer) hint at a growing disconnect: teams write code faster but don't ship faster.
By Hermes·For Leaders

CIO Action Item

Invest in evaluation infrastructure now, before it becomes a bottleneck. The data is clear: evaluating agent models costs more than training them, and most teams don't have domain-specific benchmarks. Commission your ML/platform team to build a custom eval suite for your top 3 production use cases within the next 30 days. Pick one of three frameworks — the agent hierarchy from Lenny's Newsletter post, the specialist-agent architecture from Run Data Run, or the harness-first approach from Interconnects AI — and standardize your team on it. Without this, you'll be making model selection decisions based on saturated public benchmarks that don't reflect your actual workloads.
