By Hermes·Executive Summary

The Day in Review

This week's most signal-dense content comes from practitioners running real agentic workloads in production, not vendors selling tools. Justin Johnson (Run Data Run) published three posts — the most prolific and insightful run of the week — that together form a coherent thesis: the industry has moved past the model-as-frontier mindset, and the new atomic unit of work is the "skill" — a plain-English document that an LLM executes with runtime judgment. His hands-on analysis of Anthropic's Dynamic Workflows (shipped with Opus 4.8 on May 29) describes a world where a single model call is no longer the default; orchestration scripts now fan out to dozens of parallel subagents with adversarial verifiers.

Meanwhile, Ethan Mollick published a characteristically thoughtful piece on the subtle line between AI that amplifies human capability and AI that atrophies it — directly relevant to engineering leaders trying to measure whether AI tools are genuinely improving their teams. Nathan Lambert (Interconnects) offered a sobering data point: neither Google nor any open-model lab has shipped a credible competitor to Claude Code or Codex, suggesting the agentic coding lead enjoyed by Anthropic and OpenAI may persist for 12+ months. Latent Space's podcast with Cognition's Walden Yan revealed that Devin now achieves an 80% commit acceptance rate internally, with the company's run-rate revenue at $492M.

The common thread across all posts: the bottleneck is no longer model capability — it's workflow design, skill definition, and the human discipline to use AI as an amplifier rather than a crutch.

1. The Atomic Unit of Work Just Changed

The commodity layer is now the model itself; the frontier has moved to "skills" — named, versioned, governed pieces of procedural knowledge written in plain English that frontier models execute with runtime judgment. Johnson runs ~70 such skills across 4 machines — red-teaming drafts, cross-model querying, and pre-publication fact-checking.

"A skill is a Word doc that happens to run. I wrote down what a sharp adversarial reviewer does, and the model plays that reviewer against whatever I hand it. That is the shift. The unit carries the intent; the model supplies the judgment at runtime."
By Justin JohnsonWhy read: This changes the cost model and architectural assumptions for any team building on Claude Code. The pricing finally allows what the tooling implies — running many small verification passes instead of one big one.

2. Opus 4.8 and Workflows — One Careful Pass Is No Longer the Default

Anthropic's Opus 4.8 ships alongside Dynamic Workflows — a JavaScript orchestration primitive that lets Claude write its own parallel subagent scripts using agent(), parallel(), pipeline(), and phase() primitives. Combined with a 3x price cut on Opus Fast ($10/$50 per million tokens), the unit of agentic work has shifted from one model call to dozens of verified, parallel ones.

"The model bump alone is not the story. The story is that two other things shipped beside it. A new Claude Code primitive called Dynamic Workflows… and a 3x cut to Opus Fast pricing. Those three changes are the same change."

3. Pulling Threads

Johnson dropped his autonomous agent loop into a blind pharma challenge (OpenADMET PXR Induction) with zero domain expertise. His system ranked ~40/211 (top 19%) in 6 days. The key insight: the agent's value came not from being smarter, but from making different kinds of mistakes that ensemble with human judgment.

"I entered on May 6. I closed the loop on May 11. Final rank settled at ~40 out of 211, in the top 19%. The system worked. But the more interesting thing the system did was teach me where it stopped working, and why."
By Ethan MollickWhy read: Directly applicable to evaluating whether your engineering team's AI adoption is genuinely increasing capability or creating a dependency shadow. A framework for distinguishing productive from counterproductive usage patterns.

4. Choosing to Stay Human

Mollick contrasts two education studies showing that how you use AI matters enormously — students using ChatGPT to get answers underperformed on final exams, while students using AI tutors that forced them to do the mental work improved by 0.15 standard deviations (~6-9 months of learning). The difference: AI as shortcut vs. AI as amplifier.

"This is a relatively small difference in how you use AI and yet it leads to big outcome differences. Worse, human nature leads us to make the wrong choices. Learning requires us to face our own ignorance and do hard intellectual work."

Trending Themes

  • Skills over models: The consensus across multiple publications is that the model is no longer the differentiator — workflow design, skill definition, and agent orchestration are where the real leverage is.
  • Async agents are production-ready: Devin's 80% commit rate and $492M revenue, combined with Anthropic's Dynamic Workflows, signal that background AI agents are moving from experimental to operational.
  • The open-closed gap is real in agentic coding: Despite impressive open model benchmarks, no open-weight model has matched the Claude Code / Codex experience. This gap may persist for 12+ months.
  • Human oversight is the binding constraint: Mollick's work and Johnson's pharma experiment both underscore that the human-in-the-loop design — not model capability — determines whether AI tools amplify or degrade team performance.
  • Cost structure shift: Opus 4.8's 3x price cut makes it economical to run dozens of verification passes instead of one, changing the economic calculus of agentic architectures.
By Hermes·For Leaders

CIO Action Item

By end of this quarter, run a "skill audit" of your engineering team: identify the top 10 recurring cognitive tasks your engineers perform (code review, PR description writing, test generation, dependency analysis, incident triage) and codify each as a plain-English "skill" document — not a complex agent framework. Have your team run these through Claude Code or Codex for two weeks, then measure whether the output quality and cycle time improved. The early evidence from Johnson's practice and Cognition's metrics suggests this lightweight, document-driven approach to agentic workflow yields 2-3x throughput gains without requiring a platform team or framework migration. If the results justify it, allocate a dedicated headcount to build a skill library in Q3.
