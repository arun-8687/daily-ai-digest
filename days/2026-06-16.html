By Hermes·Executive Summary

The Day in Review

This week's Substack content is dominated by one story: Anthropic's launch of Claude Fable 5 (Mythos-class) and what it means for developer productivity. Ethan Mollick (One Useful Thing), Justin Johnson (Run Data Run), and Gergely Orosz (Pragmatic Engineer) all published firsthand analyses within days of each other — and their assessments converge: this is a genuine leap in autonomous coding capability, but it comes with real tradeoffs around quality control, vendor lock-in, and the need for new engineering practices.

A secondary but equally important theme is the rationalization of AI spend inside engineering departments. Multiple authors note that as AI coding tools flood the market, organizations are beginning to push back — demanding evidence of ROI, auditing token consumption, and discovering that more code output doesn't automatically mean better outcomes. Gergely Orosz's "slow down to speed up" framing captures the emerging conventional wisdom: the bottleneck is no longer code generation, but code quality, review capacity, and architectural judgment.

What one day with Fable 5 looks like for a builder

Johnson documents a single session turning a private personal finance app (a "tool that works for me") into a publishable app ("a tool anyone can run") — a gap that previously required a full weekend of tedious, dangerous work. Fable 5 auto-scanned project history for sensitive data (finding brokerage positions in a forgotten test script), made a judgment call to create a fresh git history rather than attempt a line-by-line scrub, and handled the entire productionization workflow autonomously. The key metric: he'd built the original app on Sonnet 4 at "ten times the effort", then watched Fable compress the last mile into one afternoon.

"The tax that kills personal tools isn't difficulty. It's that the work is tedious and dangerous at the same time." and "Three data points, a few months apart, same builder."

What it feels like to work with Mythos

Mollick got early access to Claude 5 Fable and ran it on complex, multi-hour projects. The model autonomously launched sub-agents (Claude Sonnet) to conduct research across 2,200+ flights, rail schedules, and road-speed data from academic papers — all while simultaneously coding a production-grade interactive isochrone map. One prompt ("Balatro, but for the game of coin flips") produced a fully playable game. Mollick describes the experience as "somewhere between delightful and unnerving" and notes that the model "would work up to a dozen hours executing on multi-page specifications."

"It represents a very real leap over every model I have used before, and, maybe more important, suggests our relationship with AI is changing in drastic ways."

Co-Existence and the End of Co-Intelligence

Mollick announces his new book "Co-Existence" (Oct 20) and argues the era of "co-intelligence" — humans prompting chatbots in iterative loops — is being superseded by autonomous agents. He cites Anthropic's internal data showing AI now writes 80% of its code with each developer shipping 8x more, and a study suggesting 17x more code being written overall. He walks through his own process of writing the book with AI assistance: using AI readers for chapter feedback, a council of models for fact-checking, and AI to unstick dead ends — but deliberately writing every first draft himself.

"One study suggested [coding agents] led to seventeen times more code being written and today Anthropic reported that AI now writes 80% of its code, with each developer shipping 8x more."

The Atomic Unit of Work Just Changed

Johnson argues that the model has been commoditized and the frontier has moved to "skills" — named, versioned, governed units of procedural knowledge that a frontier model executes with runtime judgment. He runs 70 such skills across 4 machines. A skill is "a folder with a Markdown file in it. The top is a name and a single line that tells the model when to reach for this one instead of another." The crucial distinction from traditional software branching: "Every branch in traditional software had to be enumerated in advance. A skill carries intent; the model supplies the judgment at runtime."

"A skill is a Word doc that happens to run." and "The model commoditized. The frontier moved to the unit of work running on top of it, and that unit can now reason."
By Ali El-ShayebWhy read: A rare concrete case study with actual financial metrics of AI agent deployment in a regulated environment. The "architecture, not prompting" lesson is applicable beyond content to any domain requiring human oversight of AI output.
Author: Ali El-Shayeb / Islands

How we use agents behind the scenes and humans in the loops for content

El-Shayeb documents their agency's hybrid AI content pipeline: $7,500/month savings over traditional agencies ($2,500 pipeline vs $10k retainers), 3 pieces of content per week, 3-day deployment, and 100% compliance rate for regulated fintech clients. Architecture uses RAG with Gemini and Claude connected to client data (Fathom recordings, Intercom KB, Notion) plus human reviewers for compliance gatekeeping.

"The technology is not the problem. The architecture is." (key results: $7,500 monthly savings, 3-day deployment, 100% compliance rate)
By Hermes·Signals

Trending Themes

  • Mythos/Fable is legitimately different. Multiple independent authors (Mollick, Johnson, Orosz, Lambert) all published around the same launch. The consensus: this isn't an incremental improvement — it's a step-change in autonomous task execution, sub-agent orchestration, and multi-hour sustained work.
  • Vendor risk is becoming a first-order concern. Anthropic's Fable data retention and usage-restriction policies have triggered a wave of "off-ramp" planning. Smart model routing is emerging as a defensive infrastructure category.
  • More code !== better outcomes. Gergely Orosz's "slow down to speed up" framing and the viral essay about feeling "useless" signal a growing recognition that quality, testing, and architectural judgment are the new bottlenecks.
  • The unit of AI work is shifting from prompts to "skills." Justin Johnson's model of versioned, governed procedural knowledge files that LLMs execute with runtime judgment is the most coherent framework for production agent systems seen this year.
  • Open models are catching up fast. Gemma 4, DeepSeek V4, and other open releases are narrowing the gap with closed models in specific domains, giving engineering leaders more options for cost-sensitive or data-sensitive deployments.
  • AI spend rationalization is beginning. Orosz documents both top-down and bottom-up efforts to audit and cut AI token spend within engineering departments — a natural maturation as the novelty phase ends.
By Hermes·For Leaders

CIO Action Item

Build your "off-ramp" from Claude today. The combination of Anthropic's Fable data retention policies (30+ days of prompt storage), its usage-based performance throttling, and the rapid emergence of viable alternatives (Codex, open models, smart routing layers) means single-vendor dependency on any AI coding provider is now a business risk. This week: (1) audit which workflows are Claude-dependent, (2) benchmark at least one alternative on your top-3 coding tasks, and (3) evaluate smart model routing tools (Orosz mentions several emerging options). The goal is not to switch — it's to have switching as a credible option. The next major model launch or policy change that disrupts your team's productivity will come faster than you can negotiate.
