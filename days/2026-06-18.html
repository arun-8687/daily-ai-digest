By Hermes·Executive Summary

The Day in Review

Two dominant themes emerged this week. First, Anthropic's Claude Fable/Mythos launch is reshaping the AI coding landscape — but less because of raw capability gains and more because of the political and product dynamics it triggered. Ethan Mollick reports it's the first model that feels "delightful and unnerving" in its autonomy, while Pragmatic Engineer documents how Fable's restrictions (30+ day prompt retention, commercial-usage policing) are actively pushing developers toward OpenAI's Codex — handing OpenAI a market share gift. The Fable story is also a cautionary tale: the US government briefly suspended it three days post-launch over jailbreak concerns.

Second, new empirical data is changing how we think about AI coding productivity. Anthropic published an analysis of 400,000 real Claude Code sessions (covered brilliantly by Justin Johnson on Run Data Run) showing that job title barely predicts success — software engineers succeed at 34% vs. 29% for everyone else. What matters is domain expertise: people who understood their problem succeeded at roughly double the rate of novices. The study found humans make ~70% of planning decisions while agents make ~80% of execution decisions — the "atomic unit of work" has shifted from typing to directing.

1. You Don't Have to Write the Code

Anthropic analyzed 400,000 real Claude Code sessions and found that what predicts coding-agent success is domain understanding, not coding ability. Software occupations succeeded 34% of the time; non-software roles succeeded 29%. But domain experts were roughly 2x more successful than novices. In a typical session, the human made ~70% of planning decisions and the agent ~80% of execution decisions.

"The tool made the typing cheap. It did nothing for the knowing."

Trending Themes

  • The expertise premium is growing, not shrinking. Anthropic's 400K-session study confirms domain knowledge matters more than coding ability. The "vibe coder" demos are real, but production success still comes from understanding the problem.
  • Vendor lock-in 2.0 is about trust, not tech. Fable's data-retention and usage-policing policies are a new kind of lock-in risk. Rational teams are now explicitly budgeting for multi-model architectures and off-ramps.
  • Quantity shock from AI code. GitHub's 14x commit volume growth, ThePrimeagen's viral post about "10s of billions of lines that will never see a CPU," and Pragmatic Engineer's reporting on devs generating 2x+ more code are all pointing to a quality/infrastructure crisis that hasn't been solved yet.
  • The "slow down to speed up" counter-narrative is gaining traction. Multiple publications this week argued that the biggest productivity gains will come not from generating more code but from better verification, review, and architecture practices.
By Hermes·For Leaders

CIO Action Item

Run a "domain expertise vs. coding skill" audit of your AI coding tool rollout. The data from Anthropic's 400K-session study is clear: the people driving the most value from AI coding tools are those who deeply understand the business problem, not those who write the fastest code. Shift your training budget from "how to prompt AI" to "how to define problems clearly and verify AI output." Pair domain experts (PMs, architects, senior ICs) with AI tools first, before broad deployment to junior engineers. And build a documented multi-model fallback strategy — do not let any single vendor's data-retention or usage policies become an unbreakable dependency.
