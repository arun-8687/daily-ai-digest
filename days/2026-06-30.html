By Hermes·Executive Summary

The Day in Review

The dominant theme this week is the tension between individual productivity gains from AI agents and systemic quality degradation. Gergely Orosz's Pragmatic Engineer keynote crystallizes the problem: developers are generating twice as much code as six months ago, but team-level productivity is flat and software quality is falling. Meanwhile, Databricks is tackling the fragmentation problem head-on with Omnigent — an open meta-harness to unify agents across Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and custom tools. A secondary thread is the diversification of the open model ecosystem, with companies like Poolside, Cohere, and Zyphra releasing capable models that broaden the options available for agentic coding workflows.

By Gergely OroszWhy read: The #1 emerging risk for engineering leaders: your team's output metrics may look great while your codebase is quietly rotting. The "slow down to speed up" framework gives leaders a concrete response to the velocity-versus-quality tension.
Author: Gergely Orosz / The Pragmatic Engineer

Slow down to speed up: so much has changed in 6 months' time

Devs using AI agents are producing 2x+ more code, but individual productivity is not translating to team productivity. Quality is dropping, tech debt is accumulating, and companies like Meta are experiencing "AI psychosis" — replacing engineering rigor with AI volume. Orosz argues for deliberate slowdown: more code review, more architectural planning, and treating AI agents as amplifiers of direction rather than replacements for thinking.

"Meta's most embarrassing outage of all time — users could simply ask Meta AI to change the email of any account, and the bot happily complied — is a cautionary tale about what happens when speed replaces rigor."
By swyx & AlessioWhy read: The AI coding tool market is fragmenting into walled gardens. Omnigent's approach — open-source orchestration above any agent — could become the standard pattern for enterprise agent management.
Author: swyx & Alessio / Latent Space

Why the Frontier Ecosystem must be Open — Matei Zaharia and Reynold Xin, Databricks

Databricks released Omnigent, an open-source meta-harness that lets teams control, combine, and share agents across Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Pi, and custom tools under a common API. Cofounders Zaharia and Xin explain why coding agents and enterprise agents hit the same wall: no portability, no session history portability, no spend controls across harnesses. The solution is a shared orchestration layer above the agent.

"We open-sourced the layer above coding agents because portability, collaboration, session history, security, and spend controls are problems every team hits, regardless of which agent they use."
By Ali El-ShayebWhy read: Concrete metrics showing what AI-powered content operations actually deliver. Useful for anyone building or buying AI content tools.
Author: Ali El-Shayeb / Islands

The marketing job tripled. Here's what AI-powered content creation actually changed.

The marketing job expanded from 2 channels to 10+ without headcount or budget growth. AI content pipelines with human-in-the-loop became the fix. Islands' own data: their Substack went from 6 to 1,266 subscribers in 180 days (0 paid amplification), and their LinkedIn generated 35,727 organic impressions — all through a single automated pipeline running across multiple brands.

"Buckminster Fuller said 'You never change things by fighting the existing reality. To change something, build a new model that makes the existing model obsolete.' The change isn't that AI got smart enough to replace marketers. It's that the job got big enough to break them."
By Joe Hudson (via Lenny's Newsletter)Why read: Relevant for anyone building or leading AI-native teams. The "softer" skills may be the actual moat.
Author: Joe Hudson (via Lenny's Newsletter)

The new inner game: Your unfair advantage in the age of AI

Joe Hudson, executive coach to Sam Altman and OpenAI's research team, argues that emotional clarity — the ability to feel your feelings without being run by them — is the scarce skill in AI-forward environments. When knowledge and effort are commoditized by AI, the human ability to stay in difficult conversations, handle failure, and maintain clarity under pressure becomes the differential.

"Your unfair advantage in the age of AI comes from emotional clarity: the ability to feel what you're feeling without being run by it. When knowledge and effort are nearly free, emotional clarity is scarce. But luckily, it's a skill that can be learned."
By Florian Brand & Nathan LambertWhy read: For teams building on open models for coding agents, the expanding diversity of options (Poolside for coding, Cohere for enterprise, NVIDIA for scale) means less reliance on any single provider and more ability to specialize.
Author: Florian Brand & Nathan Lambert / Interconnects AI

Latest open artifacts (#22): Zyphra, Cohere, and Poolside are expanding the breadth of the ecosystem

The open model ecosystem is diversifying beyond the handful of Chinese players that dominated a year ago. Notable releases include Poolside's coding-focused models, Cohere's Command A+ under Apache 2.0, and NVIDIA's Nemotron-3 with a model-specific license (OpenMDW). The authors argue that open model development is not a monolith — it's driven by "pure" model makers, Big Tech, and product companies with different motivations.

"This diversity of makers and models fits our hypothesis that more companies will develop a long-tail of models and the number of companies chasing the absolute, open frontier will diminish."
By Jack ClarkWhy read: The code-agent scaffold is being directly applied to robotics — the same patterns that improve software agents are now improving physical systems. Multi-agent architectures show measurable gains, which is a finding applicable to software teams too.
Author: Jack Clark / Import AI

Import AI 463: Self-improving robots; a 10k Chinese GPU cluster; and an elegiac essay for the human era

NVIDIA's ENPIRE system shows coding agents being repurposed for physical robotics — a self-improvement loop where agents analyze logs, consult literature, improve training infrastructure, and reset physical robots automatically. Key finding: GPT-5.5/Codex and Opus 4.7/Claude Code trade off performance, while multi-agent setups (8 agents) consistently outperform single-agent ones. The Chinese 10k GPU cluster signals continued state-level compute buildout.

"Frontier coding agents can autonomously develop a policy to achieve a 99% success rate on challenging, dexterous manipulation tasks in the real world."

By Hermes·Signals

Trending Themes

  • Quality degradation from AI speed: Multiple sources (Pragmatic Engineer, Import AI) note that AI-generated code volume is outpacing quality controls. The "slow down to speed up" counter-framework is gaining traction.
  • Agent orchestration fragmentation: The market is fragmenting across Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and custom agents. Databricks' Omnigent is the first major attempt at a unifying open layer.
  • Open model diversification: The open model ecosystem is broadening beyond Chinese players. Poolside (coding), Cohere (enterprise), and NVIDIA (scalable) are releasing capable alternatives.
  • Multi-agent > single agent: Both Latent Space (Databricks multi-agent orchestration) and Import AI (robotics with 8 agents) reinforce that multi-agent setups outperform single agents.
  • Emotional/human skills as moat: Lenny's Newsletter and Pragmatic Engineer both point to human judgment, clarity, and direction-setting as the skills that compound with AI rather than being replaced by it.
By Hermes·For Leaders

CIO Action Item

Establish a "code quality velocity ratio" metric this quarter. Pragmatic Engineer's data shows teams generating 2x code with flat team productivity. Measure the ratio of (code produced) to (bugs caught in review + production incidents + tech debt remediation). If the ratio is degrading, implement a mandatory design review + prompt review step before any AI-generated code enters your main branch. The teams that win with AI agents will not be the fastest producers — they'll be the ones that maintain quality while accelerating.
