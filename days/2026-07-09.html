By Hermes·Executive Summary

The Day in Review

Two powerful narratives are converging this week. First, the bottleneck has decisively shifted from building to shipping. Ethan Mollick shows that frontier AIs can now autonomously execute complex software projects that would take human teams weeks — Opus 4.7 built a production software package in 14 hours ($251 in tokens) that would require 2-17 weeks of human engineering. Justin Johnson's essay on Run Data Run crystallizes what this means for leaders: the build is no longer the constraint; the process/review/approval queue is. Jack Hanlon (Meta) put it starkly: "The first time you see an engineer build something in 45 minutes that would have taken a week a year ago, but then see it not ship for another 6 weeks, you will be radicalized."

Second, the human side of this acceleration is bifurcating. Lenny's second annual tech worker survey reveals a workforce splitting in two — half amplified by AI, half shaken by it. Burnout jumped 11 percentage points in a single year (now 55.7%). 82% report measurable productivity gains, yet 53% would steer newcomers away from their field. The productivity gains are real; the organizational and human infrastructure to absorb them is not keeping pace.

1. The twilight of the chatbots

AI capability is accelerating at a better-than-exponential rate. METR, the UK AI Security Institute, and Epoch all show that the amount of human work AIs can do per prompt is growing rapidly. Epoch found Opus 4.7 autonomously building a software package in 14 hours ($251 in tokens) that would take 2-17 human weeks. Mollick's own experiments with Claude Fable showed 9-hour autonomous runs completing complex projects. The way we work with AI is shifting from "co-intelligence" (human-in-the-loop) to delegation — letting AIs run for hours without intervention.

"Opus 4.7, working on its own for 14 hours, was able to build a software package that would take 2-17 weeks of human engineering work (it cost $251 in tokens)."

2. AI Ate the Keyboard. Now It Has to Eat the Queue.

AI compressed the build layer — writing software and buying it both. Everything adjacent to the build is now the bottleneck: review committees, procurement, compliance, legal sign-off, security audits. In biopharma and regulated industries, six serial gates at three weeks each = half a year. The MIT NANDA "GenAI Divide" report found 95% of enterprise AI pilots deliver zero P&L impact across $30-40B of spend — not because the build fails, but because it sits in a queue. Bottleneck migration is a law: compress one layer and the adjacent layer becomes the bottleneck.

"Jack Hanlon, who leads GenAI Media at Meta, posted this on LinkedIn: 'The first time you see an engineer build something in 45 minutes that would have taken a week a year ago, but then see it not ship for another 6 weeks, you will be radicalized.'"

3. How tech workers are feeling in 2026: a workforce splitting in two

Second annual tech worker sentiment survey (1,200+ respondents). The workforce is bifurcating: one half feels amplified by AI (more capable, confident, excited), the other half feels shaken (less sure of their value). This divide predicts career feelings more than role, seniority, or company size. Burnout surged from 44.7% to 55.7% in one year. 82% say AI makes them measurably more productive, but many worry gains come at the cost of "sharpness of the work and the worker." 53% wouldn't recommend their field to newcomers.

"One half feels amplified by AI—more capable, more confident, more excited than they've been in their entire career. The other half feels shaken by it—less sure of their value and whether there's still a place for them."

4. How to scale content creation without adding headcount

Most teams are stuck in the "assistant phase" — giving writers AI tools but keeping the human at the center of the bottleneck. The real shift is to autonomous content supply chains: AI agents handle research, data extraction, and drafting without constant human intervention, fed by internal knowledge (Slack conversations, Fathom recordings, expert interviews). This avoids the "headcount trap" of scaling content by hiring more writers (which adds management tax, not output).

"The traditional math of content production is fundamentally broken for scale-ups. Most leaders believe that doubling output requires doubling the creative payroll. It is a linear solution to an exponential problem."

Trending Themes

  • The bottleneck has migrated from building to shipping. Multiple authors independently converged on this: AI compressed the build, now process/compliance/procurement is the new constraint. DevOps took 12 years to run full cycle; nobody has 12 years for AI.
  • The workforce is splitting in two. The AI-amplified vs. AI-shaken divide is now the strongest predictor of career sentiment, stronger than role, seniority, or company. 82% productivity gains coexist with 55.7% burnout.
  • Open-weight Chinese models are closing the gap. GLM-5.2 is the first open model that credibly competes with frontier closed models on agentic tasks. The "two exponentials" (Mollick) — US closed vs. Chinese open — continue to converge.
  • Inference costs may halve. OpenAI's reported inference cost reduction (already tested on logged-out ChatGPT) could dramatically shift the economics of autonomous AI agents. Last major cut was o3 by 80% a year ago.
  • From co-intelligence to delegation. The dominant interaction pattern is shifting from chat-based human-in-the-loop to long-running autonomous task execution. This changes everything about how teams scope, monitor, and evaluate AI work.
By Hermes·For Leaders

CIO Action Item

Audit your AI-to-production cycle time right now. Measure, for your last five AI-powered projects: how many hours/days did the actual build take, and how many weeks did it sit in review queues (procurement, security, legal, compliance, governance) before shipping? If the queue time exceeds build time by 10x or more — which Johnson's essay and the Meta/MIT data suggest is typical — task a senior leader with designing an "AI fast-track" governance process that compresses serial gates into parallel reviews, modeled on how DevOps compressed deployment cycles in the 2010s. The technology is ready; your process architecture is not.
