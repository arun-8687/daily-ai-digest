1. The twilight of the chatbots

AI capability is increasing at a better-than-exponential rate — Opus 4.7 running for 14 hours autonomously built a software package that would take 2–17 weeks of human engineering work ($251 in tokens). The dominant paradigm is shifting from co-intelligence (chatbots you prompt step-by-step) to agent assignment (you manage, the AI executes). At OpenAI, a quarter of workers now run at least 4 agents simultaneously every week, and non-tech functions (legal, HR) have adopted agents at nearly the same rate as engineers.

"The walls between 'coder' and 'non-coder' dissolve. The walls between 'expert' and 'novice' get higher, because the thing that makes the agent useful is knowing whether the output is right, and knowing whether the output is right comes from ten years of doing the work by hand."

2. Impressions from visiting OpenAI, Anthropic, & Cursor

Based on onsite visits to all three companies, Orosz identifies four converging trends: (1) cloud agents are the next mega-trend, with all three going all-in; (2) at OpenAI, >95% of non-engineers use Codex over ChatGPT — coding harnesses are spreading beyond engineering; (3) engineering work at Anthropic and Cursor is shifting to building environments for agents; (4) companies are aggressively optimizing spend-per-token as AI usage scales.

"OpenAI, Anthropic, and Cursor are all-in on cloud agents and expect demand for them to increase massively. [...] Claude Managed Agents is a large, complex project built over six months — a hosted service to execute long-running agents on various cloud providers."

3. The Harness Is the Moat

The model is the commodity layer you can order off a price sheet; the harness (rules, skills, memory, learning loops, continuity, always-on agents) is the durable advantage. Johnson open-sources his full harness (Claudelicious) as proof — 48 packaged skills, four memory tiers, ~70K documents of accumulated context, 8 agents across a mesh of machines.

"Picking a model is a decision you will remake four times before this sentence feels dated. It is the commodity layer: you can buy it, you can switch it, and so can the company across the street. [...] A model is a thing you rent for an hour. A harness is a thing you live inside."
By Justin Johnson, Run Data RunWhy read: This changes how you hire, train, and deploy AI tools. The most valuable skill in an AI-augmented team is not coding — it's deep domain judgment.
Author: Justin Johnson, Run Data Run

4. You Don't Have to Write the Code

Anthropic analyzed 400,000 real Claude Code sessions. The finding that matters: job title barely predicts success (software engineers: 34% verified success rate, everyone else: 29%), but domain expertise doubles it (novices: 15%, experts: 28–33%). The human makes ~70% of planning decisions; the agent makes ~80% of execution decisions. Expert advantage shows most in failure recovery — novices abandon 19% of the time vs. 5–7% for domain experts.

"The tool made the typing cheap. It did nothing for the knowing. [...] The expert hit the same wall the novice did. Then they routed around it, reframed the problem, and caught the fluent, confident, completely wrong answer before it shipped. The novice hit the wall and quit."
By Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic EngineerWhy read: Meta's disaster is a cautionary tale for moving too fast with AI-generated, AI-reviewed code without proper safeguards. The "slow down to speed up" thesis is directly actionable for engineering leaders.
Author: Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer

A post-keynote deepdive covering Meta's catastrophic "zero auth" AI-coded bug (account takeover via Meta AI), how engineering has changed since November 2025 (arrival of Opus 4.5/GPT-5.4-class agents), and emerging trends: individual productivity up but team productivity flat, "tokenmaxxing" and tooling adoption, vanishing middle management, CEOs/CTOs returning to coding, falling software quality due to AI slop.

"Engineers at the company told me this disaster was caused by AI-generated, AI-reviewed code, along with layoffs, and by forced reassignments from Integrity teams [...] onto AI labeling."

Lambert identifies GLM-5.2 as crossing a capability threshold for open-weight agentic coding. This is the first open model that can meaningfully compete with frontier closed models for agentic coding tasks, with implications for cost structure, deployment flexibility, and supply chain resilience.

"A capability threshold I've been carefully monitoring."

7. What it feels like to work with Mythos

Mollick's early-access experiments with Claude 5 Fable (Mythos-class): it works up to 12 hours autonomously on multi-page specifications. In one example, it built an interactive isochrone map by launching multiple parallel AI agents to research 2,200+ flights, rail schedules, and road speeds while simultaneously coding — then spawned adversarial agent groups to verify its own work. The experience is "delightful and unnerving."

"The AI launched multiple other AIs to help it conduct research [...] ultimately retrieving over 2,200 specific flights, the rail schedules for trains from the TGV to the Shinkansen, and road speeds per country from multiple academic papers. And while those agents were running, it started coding."

Trending Themes

  • Cloud agents are the new platform bet. OpenAI (Ona/Gitpod acqui-hire), Anthropic (Claude Managed Agents, 6-month build), and Cursor (Cloud Agents + iOS app) are all-in. The era of running agents on your laptop is ending.
  • Harness > Model. Multiple authors independently conclude that the durable competitive advantage is in the system, tools, memory, and workflows around the model — not the model itself.
  • Domain expertise is the new scarce resource. The 400K-session Anthropic study confirms what many suspected: job title doesn't predict AI-assisted success, but deep domain knowledge does by 2x.
  • Cost optimization is hitting a tipping point. AI token spend is large enough that platform teams are being formed to optimize per-token cost — a new organizational function.
  • The exponential is outrunning institutions. Mollick: "Every AI policy written before winter 2025 is already obsolete." The rate of capability improvement is faster than organizational adaptation.
  • Non-engineers are becoming coders through harnesses. At OpenAI, >95% of non-engineers use Codex. The percentage is rising across tech.
By Hermes·For Leaders

CIO Action Item

Conduct a "harness audit" this quarter. Your teams likely have varying levels of agent tooling (MCP servers, skills libraries, memory systems, cloud agent infrastructure) — but almost none of it is standardized, shared, or treated as organizational IP. Assign a platform engineer to catalog what's working, identify the 80/20 harness components (rules, context, memory, skills) that could be shared across teams, and spin up a prototype cloud agent environment. The companies that treat their harness as a moat — not the model they happen to be renting this month — will win the next 24 months. Simultaneously, review your AI governance and code review pipelines: Meta's "zero auth" disaster is a $0 lesson in what happens when AI-generated code bypasses human security review.
